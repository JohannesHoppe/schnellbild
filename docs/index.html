What it does

Look at it large, page with the keys

Space opens a picture as large as the window allows, ◀ / ▶ page through the folder. Zoom in with the trackpad or + / −, turn with ⌥ + ◀ / ⌥ + ▶, full screen with f. Animated GIFs and SVG included, PDFs page by page.

A slideshow (s) runs through the folder and all its subfolders, photos and videos alike. Whatever the slideshow plays is the playlist: p opens it to reorder and weed out.

Your photos on the map and the globe

L goes from the grid to the list and on to the Atlas. It shows the photos and videos of a folder and its subfolders where they were taken. Zoomed far out, the map is a globe in front of the stars, with day and night if you like and the cities lit on its night side; zoom in and it flattens into the map.

  • The timeline lays the trip out by time: drag across it, and the map shows only that part
  • T plays the tour: the map glides from place to place, the photos show large, the videos play a few seconds
  • The map is inside the app: nothing goes online, no location leaves your Mac

Videos, also the ones macOS can't play

What macOS plays itself (MP4, MOV, AVCHD …) plays through macOS, HDR included; everything else through the VLC engine built in: MKV, WebM, AVI, WMV, FLV, MPEG, DVD video.

  • ◀ / ▶ jump a tenth of the video, ⇧ + ◀ / ⇧ + ▶ a hundredth
  • ▲ / ▼ set the volume, for every video
  • ⌘ + ▶ to the next video and ⌘ + ◀ back: the first goes on where it was left
  • Subtitles inside the video (V) show from the start; switched on halfway, the line already running appears at once, and ASS subtitles keep their position, colours and fonts
  • Finder and Quick Look get thumbnails for MKV, WebM, WMV, FLV and VOB too

Archives open like folders

ZIP, RAR and 7z, comics (CBZ, CBR) included, also in several parts; ZIP and RAR also with a password. Nothing is unpacked to disk, and videos play straight from the archive.

Sort your holiday photos like in Norton Commander

D shows two folders side by side. Mark pictures, then F5 copies and F6 moves them to the other side, or drag them there.

Rename, new folder, trash, bookmarks, and undo for renaming, trashing, copying and moving.

Search through every subfolder

The search field matches the names of folders, archives, pictures and videos, in the folder shown and everything below it. Results arrive while it searches, a slow network share included. Ignoring case, matching it, or a regular expression.

Better pictures, files untouched

E improves exposure, contrast, saturation and sharpness automatically, for photos and videos alike. A opens the sliders. It is a view: your files stay as they are.

What the file really is

I or ⌘ + I opens the file info beside the picture: when and where it was taken, with a small map and the town nearby, camera and lens, format, resolution, codecs, bitrate, bit depth and colour profile, and every metadata key (EXIF, GPS, IPTC, XMP, QuickTime) to filter and copy. Times and places also come from XMP sidecars, a GoPro's or DJI drone's GPS data, Sony's clip files and GPX tracks in the folder.

A file whose extension doesn't match its content gets a note. Files whose name says nothing count by their content, and downloads still in progress (.part, .crdownload …) show as far as they have arrived.

Every key at hand

F1 lists every key, to print too; K shows a small keyboard to click. Set your own keys, or pick the keys as in Phiewer. Grid, list or Atlas (L), sort by name, kind, date, size or distance from a place, several windows, and a boss key (X).

Light or dark, your choice

Some swear by dark mode, others can't stand it. So you decide: Same as System, Light or Dark, in Settings (⌘ + ,).

Safe with files from others

Pictures, videos and archives from strangers are exactly what a viewer is for, so Schnellbild treats every file as hostile, out of the box.

  • In Apple's App Sandbox. No network at all, starts no programs by itself, sees only the folders you open to it plus Pictures. macOS enforces this, so we couldn't cheat even if we wanted to: the app simply can't reach the internet.
  • Only reading. Looking at something never changes it and never unpacks it to disk.
  • Videos can't go online. A "video" that is really a playlist pointing to the internet plays nothing.
  • Double click opens documents, not programs. Apps, scripts and installers never, even when they hide behind a picture's name.
  • Signed and notarized, with Apple's Hardened Runtime.
  • Provably built from the public source. GitHub signs where every download comes from: the commit and the build. Anyone can check it.
